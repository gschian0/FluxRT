# Symbiosis and Sweat: The Single-Operator Broadcast

### Prepared by Gennaro Schiano

## I. Abstract & Concept Statement
This project is an adaptation of live internet television and internet radio functioning as a real-time media processor. It is a system that filters, steers, generates, and broadcasts content live to platform, demonstrating the absolute limits of what a single human operator can sustain when supported by managed cloud infrastructure.

The work explores the paradoxical romance between the independent creator and the corporate tech ecosystem. I love the corporation: it provides the L4 GPUs, the managed cloud, and the architecture that makes global broadcast possible. But the corporation demands a toll. It provides the infrastructure to eat, but guarantees you will drown in sweat to do so. This piece makes that physical and economic reality visible, presenting ephemeral media not as a finished artifact, but as an unstable, exhausting, and continually produced live event.

## II. The Apparatus: Loving the Corporate Machine
The technical foundation of this work embraces the beauty of corporate infrastructure. By combining AI generation, live processing, and cloud-based orchestration into a single pipeline, the project relies on the bleeding-edge efficiencies of modern computing.

The power of a single L4 GPU in this ecosystem is not in replacing artistic labor, but in making that labor feasible at scale. As the industry races toward increasingly massive GPUs designed to crunch models at lower bit rates, such as 1.5-bit and FP4, this project utilizes that exact corporate optimization to run a global broadcast. The architecture is flawless, sterile, and highly managed, a stark contrast to the human effort required to steer it.

## III. The Physical Toll: Unmanaged Labor in the Managed Cloud
While the cloud infrastructure is managed, the human labor is decidedly not. The core tension of the performance is the friction between infinite algorithmic generation and finite human endurance.

To operate this real-time media processor, to constantly shape image, audio, and generation in motion, is an act of extreme physical and mental labor. The corporate promise of AI is seamless automation and ease; the reality for the independent artist is a hyper-accelerated treadmill. The work argues that while the institution and the gatekeepers offer the tools for survival, they simultaneously extract every ounce of the operator’s bandwidth. The resulting broadcast is a testament to the sweat required to feed the machine.

## IV. Curatorial Provocation
This project challenges the museum to step away from the static artifact and support the living system itself. The style guide and pipeline logic are determined entirely by the operator in the trenches, not by better-financed gatekeepers or institutional boards.

To exhibit this work is to exhibit the process, the maintenance, the operational knowledge, and the live authorship that makes it possible. The museum is invited to host not a stream or a tool, but the labor of the broadcast, the real-time, grueling symbiosis of man and cloud.

## V. Pipeline Dreams
The long-term vision for this project is a 3D real-time broadcasting pipeline that can grow beyond the current live stream framework. In that next stage, the system would incorporate AI models for equirectangular rendering and for compositing 3D point clouds as Gaussian splats, turning the broadcast into a spatial, immersive media environment.

This pipeline dream keeps the same core philosophy: a single operator directing a live system through managed infrastructure, but now expanded into a more dimensional and experimental form. The goal is not merely to stream content, but to build a broadcast engine capable of handling camera inputs, spatial data, and synthesized 3D media in real time.

## VI. Local TTS
As a near-term extension, the project will add local text-to-speech as a live performance layer. The voice system should remain expressive and playable, with control over timing, delay, reverb, chorus, and other spatial effects so that speech becomes part of the broadcast instrument rather than a flat narration channel.

The emphasis is on local processing and immediate responsiveness: a voice engine that can be steered live by the operator, integrated into the same real-time pipeline as video and music, and tuned to match the emotional and mechanical character of the work.
